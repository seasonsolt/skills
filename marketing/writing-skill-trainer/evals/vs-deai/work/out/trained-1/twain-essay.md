# My Doctor Asked How the Standing Is Going

When I turned fifty-one, my doctor advised me to stand more, and I took the advice in the spirit in which it was given, which is to say I spent 640 dollars on it. A man who buys a motorized desk has made a serious commitment. A man who buys a "smart" one has made it in writing.

The desk is controlled by an app. The app requires an account, and the account requires a password of at least fourteen characters. I chose one with care. I mention the password so that no one will say I entered into this lightly.

On the first morning the desk rose to its full height of 48 inches, in a smooth and confident manner, like a man who has been told the meeting is his. I admired it. I had never seen furniture take a doctor's advice so promptly. Then I asked it to come down, and it declined, and it went on declining for three days.

I want to be fair to the desk. It had been asked to stand, and it stood. My own doctor has never been able to say as much for his patients. If anyone in the household had a record of perfect compliance, it was the desk, and I felt a certain pride in it, as one does in a nephew who has done well in an unrelated field.

Naturally, work does not stop because a desk has become a landmark. I had correspondence to answer, and the correspondence was now forty-eight inches up in the air, and I was not. I solved this as any practical person would. I fetched a kitchen chair, climbed onto it, and answered my letters standing, at last, in the full sense my doctor intended, and somewhat beyond it. I will add only that I was never more upright than on that chair, and never so carefully so.

On the third day I telephoned the company's help line, where a patient voice told me to "restart the furniture." I have been told many things by professionals, but I had not before been asked to restart anything I could sit on. I thanked the voice. I did not restart the furniture, as I could not think how one begins, or what the furniture would say about it afterward.

It was my neighbor, Mrs. Abernathy, a retired schoolteacher, who came over, looked at the desk for some time, and pulled the plug out of the wall and put it back. The desk came down at once. It had the manner of a child who has been spoken to by someone with experience. I do not say that the app, the account and the fourteen characters were of no use. I say only that none of them was Mrs. Abernathy.

I work at the desk now sitting down, at its lowest setting, which I believe is what its makers had always secretly hoped for. The desk and I are on better terms than we have ever been. My doctor has asked how the standing is going. I told him it is going well, and I think this is accurate, since for three days it went extremely well, and no one can say it has not gone since.
