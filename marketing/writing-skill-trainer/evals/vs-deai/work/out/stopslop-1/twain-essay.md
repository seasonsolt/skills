# The Desk That Would Not Sit

I am fifty-one years old, and my doctor told me to stand more. I took her advice to a store and came home with a motorized standing desk that cost 640 dollars. The box called it smart. I believed the box.

The desk takes orders from an app. The app wants an account. The account wants a password of at least 14 characters. I have a mortgage, a car and a drawer of tax returns, and none of them ever asked me for 14 characters. I typed my street, then my first pet, then a long sentence about my feelings toward the app. The app accepted the sentence. I should have taken that as a warning.

On the first morning I pressed the button and the desk rose to its full height of 48 inches. There it stayed. I pressed the button again. I pressed the app. I pressed the desk itself, with my palm, the way you press a horse. For three days it held at 48 inches like a flagpole with nothing to fly.

During those three days I answered my correspondence standing on a kitchen chair. My doctor had not pictured this posture. The chair wobbled and the laptop slid toward the edge, and every email I wrote read like a speech from a ledge. I kept them short. A man on a chair has no patience for a long reply.

I called the company's help line. A calm voice heard my story and told me to "restart the furniture." I asked her to say it again, and she said it again. I have restarted computers, routers and a thermostat, but nobody had ever asked me to restart a piece of furniture. I stood on my chair in the quiet and tried to work out which part of a desk is its brain. I patted the top. It gave me nothing.

On the third day my neighbor, Mrs. Abernathy, a retired schoolteacher, saw me through the window on my chair. She came in without a word and looked at the desk. Then she pulled the plug out of the wall and put it back. The desk hummed and came down to a height I could use. She did not ask for the password. She gave me the look a teacher gives a student who has skipped the instructions, and she went home.

I stood there with 640 dollars of equipment, a 14-character password and the sense that one of us had just been taught a lesson. It was not the desk.

Now I work sitting down. The desk sits at its lowest setting, where it is a table, and I have grown fond of it. It is an expensive table, and it no longer surprises me. I do not open the app. My account is out there somewhere with my long sentence in it, waiting for me to need it.

My doctor has asked how the standing is going. I looked at the desk, then at the chair I was sitting on, then at the lowest setting. I told her it is going well. The desk and I have not discussed it further.
